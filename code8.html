HTML Character Entities

1. Mathematical Symbols

Less than: <

Greater than: >

Multiplication: ×

Division: ÷

Plus or minus: ±

Infinity: ∞

Not equal to: ≠

Square root: √

Pi: π

Sum: ∑

2. Copyright and Trademark Symbols

Copyright: © 2026

Registered Trademark: ®

Trademark: ™

3. Quotation Marks

Double Quotes: "Hello World"

Single Quotes: 'Hello World'

Left Double Quote: “Welcome”

Left Single Quote: ‘HTML’

4. Angle Brackets

Opening Bracket: <

Closing Bracket: >

HTML Example: <h1>Hello World</h1>

5. Other Special Characters

Ampersand: &

Non-breaking Space: Hello World

Euro Symbol: €

Cent Symbol: ¢

Degree Symbol: 25°C

Bullet Symbol: •

Paragraph Symbol: ¶

Section Symbol: §

Em Dash: —

Ellipsis: …

6. Character References and Their Uses

  • &lt; displays the less-than symbol (<).
  • &gt; displays the greater-than symbol (>).
  • &amp; displays the ampersand (&).
  • &copy; displays the copyright symbol (©).
  • &quot; displays double quotation marks (").
  • &nbsp; creates a non-breaking space.

7. Why Character Entities Are Useful

HTML character entities display special characters correctly. They prevent angle brackets from being interpreted as HTML tags and allow mathematical symbols, quotation marks, copyright signs, and other special characters to appear on a webpage.
